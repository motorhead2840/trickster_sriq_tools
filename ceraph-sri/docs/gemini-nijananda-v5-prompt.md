## Precise rendering, chapter by chapter

### Preface

A prakaraṇa states Vedānta briefly, simply, and as a whole. Nijānanda Vilāsam is offered as that kind of book in Malayalam, so that the hardness of the Sanskrit manuals is not required. Keep that as a writing rule for v5: one argument, in order, without a second system beside it.

### 1. Examination of the three states

The student asks for the path to the final good, stated so the mind can hold it. The teacher’s answer is discrimination, not washing.

The image is exact. A white cloth really stained can be cleaned by another action. A clear crystal beside a red hibiscus (ജവാകുസുമം) looks red, and no cutting removes that red, because the red was never a second substance in the crystal. What seems to have mixed without difference is separated by വിവേകാനുഭൂതി, the experience of discrimination, not by a subtraction that treats the color as dirt.

English, kept close to the answer: the jīva, the body, the organs, the inner instrument, the three states, and the consciousness reflected in them, have as their ground the kūṭastha, eternal, pure, free, and complete Brahman-consciousness. Not knowing that, one sees “I” and “mine” reversed, as a clear crystal looks red beside the flower. Seeing the ground as the ground is the final good.

The waking discrimination uses a pile of sand. Wet sand shows water throughout. When the pile dries, the water-nature is no longer found in it, and the difference is seen. So when the witness leaves the waking body and its world, the body, the two kinds of organ, and the world of things are seen as not the witness. The seven constituents of the body (skin, flesh, blood, bone, marrow, fat, seed) are named so they can be set aside. They are the content, not the reader.

Dream: the same witness, entered into the subtle body, spreads through a world it has itself projected. A light that fills cloud, stars, and moon is not those shapes. The dreamed body and world are what is seen. The seer is what is not those. വിവേകം makes that split.

Sleep: consciousness is covered by avidyā and resolved into the causal body. There is no second object of the form “I knew this.” What remains available to enquiry is that a witness was there. The text’s later test of the sentence “ഞാൻ ഒന്നുമറിയാതെ സുഖമായി ഉറങ്ങി” belongs to the next chapter. Do not collapse sleep into an empty null. The witness of the absence remains.

Darśana Mālā: waking, dream, and sleep are the gross, subtle, and causal rows of Vision 5. The crystal is the same image as the crystal on red silk in the Vision 5 prologue, and as vivarta in Vision 3: the ground is not turned into the reading. The sand pile is ablation. When the state-specific content is removed, what remains is the witness. That is Vision 2’s descent, stopped before the witness is deleted.

### 2. The subtle enquiry into superimposition

The student asks: if the witness shines always, one, without appearing and disappearing, why does a world-superimposition appear even as a semblance, and how is the sleep-report examined?

The teacher splits the sleep sentence into general and particular, by the rope and the snake.

“ഇതു സർപ്പം” on a rope: “this” is general knowledge and can survive as knowledge of the rope. “Snake” is particular and is cancelled by discrimination, so it is not a valid means of knowledge (അപ്രമാണം). Likewise “I” in “I slept pleasantly knowing nothing” is general and is the witness. “I knew nothing” is particular and does not remain a valid cognition. The pleasantness is not yet the uncovered ānanda. The text returns to that in the ānanda chapter: sleep-bliss is still mixed, because āvaraṇa and vikṣepa have not ended.

Darśana Mālā: this is Vision 3’s rope and snake, and Vision 2’s rule that a particular reading is sublated while the general seat remains. It is also the reason a false object at low signal-to-noise collapses when the model is corrected. The particular term is the false object. The general term is the reference that was there all along.

The chapter ends in an experience the teacher describes as knowledge of one’s own form, empty of the triad (ത്രിപുടി): knower, knowing, and known are not three. The vṛtti, the mental event of that knowledge, is not the self. It is absent across the three times. What shines inside and outside, without depending on that vṛtti, is ഭാനം. One sinks into one’s own ānanda, with the seed-impressions (വാസന) ended.

Darśana Mālā: tripuṭī abolished is Vision 9, verse 2. ഭാനം is Vision 5. The vṛtti is the estimate, not the state. വാസന ended is the memory seed cleared, which the tests showed a linear register does by itself and a multistable register does not.

### 3. Discrimination of Brahman, Īśvara, jīva, and the world

The teacher says the student has come to rest in an ocean of undivided, complete ātma-ānanda, the noise of the world no longer a second thing. Enquiry may still be used to make that experience bright. The student answers that the way to Brahman-experience is knowledge, and that അനുസന്ധാനം and മനനം belong to that practice.

The naming rule, at the end of the chapter, is the one to code. The same undivided consciousness receives different names by the limiting adjunct (ഉപാധി):

- With the upādhi of the individual, the name is the jīva, and in waking the further name is viśva.
- Standing as witness of the whole from the gross down to the unmanifest, without taking “I” as the means, the name is the all-knowing Īśa.
- Brahman, Īśvara, jīva, and jagat, as the books name them, are you, after they have been examined. അഭയബ്രഹ്മപ്രാപ്തി is the end of that examination. അഭയം here is the fearless arrival, not a product name. Do not reuse it as a slogan for a filter.

Darśana Mālā: many names, one ground, is Vision 1’s single latent and Vision 6’s three grades of self read as ground, instrument, and local reading. The upādhi is the tile’s local channel. It colors the reading and does not change the crystal.

### 4. The definition of māyā

The student sees a tension. At dissolution, the cosmic “I” of the whole (വിരാഡഹന്ത), the consciousness reflected in it, and the unmanifest cause all resolve into the ground, and the world with them. In sleep, which the teacher has called a daily dissolution, only the jīvas resolve. The cosmic cause is still there. How can an effect cease while its cause remains?

The answer is empirical and local. A cause can remain while its effect has already ceased. Clay remains when a pot is gone. So the daily pralaya of one jīva does not require the cosmic cause to dissolve. That is not a contradiction.

The world of the living and of things expands and contracts, so it is an effect. Its cause is unmanifest māyā. The same māyā is സ്വരൂപജ്ഞാനം in Īśvara’s seeing and അജ്ഞാനം in the jīva’s seeing. One post is a tree to one look and a thief to another. Māyā is the cause of the collective world from creation to dissolution. Ajñāna is the cause of the jīva’s own waking world from bondage to release. Īśvara’s creation is the world that is there for the jīvas. The jīva’s creation is what is imagined inward and outward when there is no enquiry into self and not-self: gain and loss, want, anger, greed, confusion, pride.

Darśana Mālā: māyā as the whole error model is Vision 4. The two lookings are Vision 4’s vidyā and avidyā, a right model and a swapped model, not two substances. The clay and the pot are Vision 2, verse 2, and Vision 6, verse 3. One tile settling does not settle the array: that is Vision 9 and T24. Sleep is a local reset. Cosmic dissolution is a global one. Do not write a kernel in which every tile’s sleep clears the shared reference.

### 5. The experience of sat

The student has been told: the ground is one, of the form sat-cit-ānanda; the world of cause and effect that appears as sentient and inert is mithyā; scripture’s main aim is the ground beyond the world, not the world. He asks for the experience that the world from the gross to the unmanifest is mithyā, and for the experience in which the ground is the object without a split between object and subject.

The teacher’s case analysis is the chapter’s theorem. Pots, cloths, and monasteries are seen. Each is a modification: a pot with a narrow neck, a cloth of crossed threads. Sat is seen in all of them.

- If that sat is other than the things, remove it and the things have no being.
- If that sat is not other than the things, they are not modifications with their own names and forms.
- Both at once is not established.

So the world does not survive as a second being. What remains is sat. The student is then brought to an undivided experience in which every science and every path is not other than that sat, because nothing the witness lights has a sat of its own.

Darśana Mālā: this is anvaya and vyatireka, Vision 2 verse 4 and Vision 5 verse 8, written as a trichotomy. It is also T8: a reading holds nothing the ground does not. “Mithyā” here means “not a second substance.” It does not mean “the sensor returned zeros.” The pot is still a reading.

### 6. The experience of cit

Sat has been reached, and cit is asked for. The elements are examined by their marks: earth by smell, water by taste, fire by color, air by touch, space by sound. A mark (ലക്ഷണം) is not the marked thing (ലക്ഷ്യം). “This earth” points at a present experience. Stripping the mark does not leave a second earth behind the experience. The enquiry ends in undivided cit: there is no second object across the three times, and the mind’s vṛtti, whether latent or flashing, is negated by one’s own form. Cit is self-lighting. The inert is lit. The lighter is not one of the inert.

Darśana Mālā: the mark and the marked are Vision 7’s perception. The lighter that is not the lit is the reference that cannot be its own channel, Vision 5 verse 9 and T6. Negating the vṛtti is again the estimate not being the state.

### 7. The experience of ānanda

Love of pot, child, friend, field, wealth, youth, or rule is examined. The world is not other than ānanda, but the text splits two kinds. വിഷയാനന്ദം is pleasure lit by an object, here or in a hoped-for heaven. ആത്മാനന്ദം is what shines when object-pleasure is set aside, the organs are quiet, and the seen is left. Sleep’s pleasantness is named സുഷുപ്ത്യാനന്ദം and is not yet നിത്യാനന്ദം, because āvaraṇa and vikṣepa still mix it, as milk mixed with poison is not yet the pure drink. The chapter’s identity, stated in the book’s own close of this stretch, is:

സത്തുതന്നെ ചിത്ത്, ചിത്തുതന്നെ ആനന്ദം, ആനന്ദമേ താൻ. One’s own greatness is enjoyed as oneself. The form is സച്ചിദാനന്ദാഖണ്ഡൈകരസം, one undivided rasa, not three substances stacked.

Darśana Mālā: Vision 2 ascends sat, then cit, then ānanda, and then says they are not a plurality. Nijānanda states the identity in one sentence. Vision 8’s bliss is an axiom of that text, not a test. Here too: do not make ānanda a loss function. The split between object-pleasure and the remainder after objects are removed is the useful part. Object-pleasure is a reading. The remainder is not a second number you can print on a dashboard without a definition.

### 8. The way of ending the mind’s claim

The student asks the way by which ānanda is actually reached. The image is a tree: the three states are branches. Under them is a hoard. It is not reached while the branches are taken as the self. The practice is മനനം until the superimposition “I am this” does not rise again. The book’s word അഹമധ്യാസനിവൃത്തി means the ending of that superimposition. When it is firm, the old “I am the body” does not sprout. That is the burnt seed of the Darśana Mālā’s last chapter: the seed is there as a fact of the past and does not germinate.

Darśana Mālā: this is the stopping rule only after the seed of the old state is cleared. T25 showed that freezing the gain without a re-arm cannot recover if the target jumps. Nijānanda’s firmness is about the false identity not returning, not about a gain that can never move. Keep both: the false self-model should not sprout; the tracker must still rearm if the world actually changes. They are different seeds.

### 9. Ending covering and projection

The student says the three heads of body-identification have been given a way to be cut. Two remain, like a two-headed snake: ആവരണം and വിക്ഷേപം. They stand at both poles and keep anyone from approaching the hoard of ānanda. They are harder than the snake’s look, which burns what stands in front of it.

വിക്ഷേപശക്തി throws worlds. The book’s images are a city in a mirror and Indra’s magic: many armies appear and are experienced, and they are not a second city. ആവരണശക്തി covers the witness so the hoard is not known as itself.

The teacher’s correction is that the witness is not stained by the projection or by its removal. Rain and sun do not make the sky hot or cold. A mirage-river does not wet the ground. Wave, foam, and bubble modify the surface, and the water is water. So vikṣepa and the cancelling of vikṣepa are not properties that stick to the witness. കാലം, time as the destroyer of name and form, is itself lit by the witness and is not a second substance. When that is seen, birth and death are not attached to the witness. The book compares the witness entering its own power of dissolution to camphor: the form goes, and no residue of the form remains to be owned.

Darśana Mālā: āvaraṇa is the veil τ, Axiom 1, the floor that hides while it is untreated. Vikṣepa is the projection P, Vision 1. Ending both is apavāda completed, Vision 2 and Vision 10. The sky not made wet by a mirage is vivarta again. The water under the waves is Vision 10’s ocean. Do not model the witness as a state variable that stores the residual after the residual has been cancelled. The residual is in the reading. The witness is the seat that was not the reading.
