WHICH RULE TO ADOPT FOR THE SRI TILE
----------------------------------------
   Recommended rule on this evidence: Mehra AKF.  Mean late regret vs the oracle, averaged across the 6 scenarios, is -0.0013.  Collapse flags (5× oracle, or recovery on fewer than half the walks, or jump-up RMS>0.3): 0 scenario(s).
   On the q×10 jump, Mehra AKF reached RMS(κ)=0.0540 ± 0.0010 versus oracle 0.0544, Kalman-at-initial-q 0.0734, and v1 Ξ 0.5949.
   Median recovery to within 1.5× the oracle was 0 steps (8/8 walks).
   On the r-mismatch test (true 10 dB, r̂ from a 20 dB formula), Mehra AKF reached RMS(κ)=0.0862 ± 0.0010 versus oracle 0.0779 and Kalman-at-initial-q 0.0955.
   Note: Mehra attributes all excess innovation power to q, so an underestimated r can push its gain *up*, which is the wrong direction for extra measurement noise. Sage–Husa adapts r as well; that difference is visible here if Sage–Husa wins this cell.
   It is not uniformly best in every scenario:
     mismatch: Sage–Husa AKF was lower (0.0813 vs 0.0862).  The recommendation is a robustness compromise, not a per-cell optimum.
   Where this rule has a slightly *lower* late RMS than the oracle (stationary, jump_up, ramp, fault), that is consistent with v2, where a Kalman filter with q×4 beat the filter that used the true q.  A slightly higher gain can help in this finite-K, gauge-aligned model.  It is not evidence that the adaptive rule is more efficient than a well-specified Kalman filter in the large-sample limit.
   Documented filter cost is about 36 flops per tile per step (both channels), versus ~20 for a fixed-q Kalman and ~72 for the two-model IMM.  These are engineering counts, not measured cycles.  The shared unveiling step (graph least-squares on the neighbour ratios) dominates any of these filter costs.
   The v1 Ξ-consistency rule is not recommended: after the q×10 jump it reached RMS(κ)=0.5949 ± 0.0617.  That rule still lowers the gain when neighbour consistency falls, which is the wrong sign.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN
----------------------------------------
   - Identifiability.  Innovation variance alone cannot separate q from r:  E[ν²] = a²P + q + r.  The Mehra estimator is unbiased for q in steady state when r̂ = r and P equals the true posterior; otherwise the bias in r̂ is absorbed into q̂.  A lag-1 Mehra correction, or a Cramér–Rao bound for this blind model, was not derived here.
   - The forgetting factor is still a tuning knob.  Sensitivity is reported, but there is no automatic λ scheduler in the recommended rule.
   - Partial overlap.  v2 showed that layout matters as soon as neighbouring tiles share only part of their inputs.  All tracking walks here used full overlap on a 64-tile torus.  The theorem-plus-filter story under sliding-window overlap is untested.
   - The gauge is still a single complex factor for the whole network, computed from the held estimate.  A chip that cannot do that global step would need a reference tile or a different pin.  That choice was left open in v2 and is still open.
   - IMM used two models at {q0, 10 q0}.  The q÷10 jump is therefore a mismatched-model test, not a matched one.  A three-model bank, or a different pair, was not tried.
   - The fault model is a single real-part step of 0.5 on one tile.  Other faults (phase steps, multiple tiles, sticking) were not tried.
   - Settling time of the *unveiling* iteration (v2 experiment D) is not revisited; every filter here was fed the converged least-squares / leading-eigenvector estimate.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
=======================
- This is a classical numerical simulation of complex arrays.  Using i and a Hilbert-space notation for a veil does not make the simulation quantum, and it does not demonstrate entanglement, a quantum advantage, or a new physical chip.
- There is no CMOS, timing-error, voltage, or energy model here.  The notebook does not compare a tile array to Razor-style detection or adaptive voltage scaling.  That measured-result claim still needs an FPGA or ASIC experiment.
- A programming model in which the programmer declares Ξ_crit(c) is not implemented here.
- Filter flop counts are documented estimates of arithmetic in the *filter*, not a chip area or clock-cycle model, and they exclude the shared unveiling solve.
