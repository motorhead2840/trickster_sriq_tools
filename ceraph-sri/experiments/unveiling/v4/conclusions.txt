EXPERIMENT B — gauge pinning
----------------------------
The network recovers only relative veils; one global complex factor is unidentifiable without a pin.
Theory: absolute error = relative error + gauge error.  With M well-separated references,
Var(gauge) ≈ (r_ref + r_rel)/M.  Under a Dirichlet pin, per-tile absolute error should grow
with effective resistance to the nearest reference; a post-hoc global α makes the leftover
error spatially flat (it only removes the common mode).

RECOMMENDED GAUGE STRATEGY
--------------------------
Mehra AKF remains the recommended on-chip tracker (it still ties the oracle on the torus q×10 case at the overlaps tested). For the gauge: if only relative (gauge-invariant) error matters, leave the global factor free and report aligned error. If a tile needs an absolute veil, pin it. One known-good reference already removes the common-mode error; several well-spread references cut remaining gauge variance roughly as 1/M (here: ring post-hoc gauge MSE fell from about 5.5e-4 at M=1 to 8.6e-5 at M=8). Post-hoc and Dirichlet pins had similar *mean* absolute error; the predicted per-tile MSE ∝ R_eff law was not visible at 20 dB on either graph, so that spatial claim is unconfirmed here. A stale pin becomes a drifting bias and must be refreshed on the OU timescale. A single faulty reference ruins a one-ref pin; a mean/LS combination of several refs degrades more gracefully with M, while a median-log combination was unstable at M=4 in this run and only clearly recovered at M=8. A soft prior that the mean of κ is zero is only a convention, not an absolute measurement.

PLAIN FAILURES AND LIMITS
-------------------------
- v1 g(Ξ) remains a negative control: it collapses on a q×10 jump at torus ρ=1.0, torus ρ=0.75, torus ρ=0.5, torus ρ=0.25, ring ρ=1.0, ring ρ=0.75, ring ρ=0.5, ring ρ=0.25.
- A soft prior that the network-mean veil is zero is not a substitute for a real reference: its absolute MSE stays close to the unpinned error (the true mean of κ is not zero).
- A single faulty reference ruins an LS/mean pin (absolute MSE=2.522e-01); with one ref there is also no median to fall back on.
- A stale reference (frozen at t=0) lets absolute RMS grow to 0.493 vs fresh 0.037; references have to be refreshed on the OU timescale.
- The predicted MSE ∝ R_eff law was not clearly visible on the ring either in this run; treat the slope as inconclusive.
- On the torus at 20 dB the resistance-vs-distance law is weak: the measurement floor dominates the small R_eff of a 4-regular grid. The ring is the diagnostic graph.
- Median-log pin was not reliably better at M=4 (MSE=4.068e-01 ± 4.058e-01 vs LS 2.002e-02); it only clearly recovered at M=8 in this run. Do not treat a four-reference median as automatically robust.
- On the ring the formula floor with K_eff=ρK underestimates the static MSE as overlap drops (at ρ=0.75, formula=9.443e-04, measured=3.142e-03). The first-order term is no longer a pure coboundary, and chip methods that trust the formula can lose the oracle on a stationary ring. The torus and expander stay close to the formula.
